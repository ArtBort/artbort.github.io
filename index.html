Streaming art on the internet

In 2017, I started opening up my creative process to the world by streaming live on Twitch. At the time, I was living in a small caravan in Belgium—a quiet, intimate space where I could let my emotions flow and connect with others. Piece by piece, like strokes of glue holding a collage together, we built our own community. It became a sanctuary where creative minds gathered to share, inspire, and simply enjoy the process of making art. Looking back, it’s amazing how much a few old photos can stir up. Today, livestreaming remains my springboard—a space that motivates me to tackle the little things that bring me joy, and to share those moments with whoever stops by.

Recycled materials

There has always been something deeply meaningful to me about finding value in what others discard. In a world driven by massive overconsumption, art materials are everywhere—you just have to look for them. One of my favorite, everyday staples is simple cardboard; it’s sturdy, resilient, and brings rich, warm brown tones to a piece. But my absolute favorite resource? Vintage magazines, particularly from the 1920s, with 1970s print as a close second. The underlying truth of my work is simple: the material is already out there. It carries its own intrinsic beauty—you just have to see it.

Surrealism

I’ve always been a surrealist at heart. The first time I encountered a surrealist painting, I was completely absorbed in a world of pure imagination—and it never truly left me. Later, when I discovered Dadaism, it left me utterly bewildered. It broke every rule, challenging my brain in a way I’d never experienced before. That revelation introduced me to complete creative freedom: the ability to make work that is entirely unique, unburdened by convention.
